DENSE SHARED-BACKBONE ENSEMBLES: WHAT AGGREGATION CHANGES
5 October 2026. Saved-prior/source-only analytic memo; no payload read or execution.

1. Three distinct operators

Write p_m=softmax(z_m). The probability obtained from mean raw logits is

    g_c=softmax(mean_m z_m)_c
       = product_m p_mc^(1/M) / sum_d product_m p_md^(1/M).

It is a normalized geometric probability pool (logarithmic opinion pool), whereas a_c=mean_m p_mc is an arithmetic pool (linear opinion pool). Their log odds differ: log(g_c/g_d)=mean_m log(p_mc/p_md); arithmetic pooling has no such identity. This product is not a justified independent-evidence likelihood merely because members have different parameters, especially with a shared backbone.

For binary scores, g=sigmoid(mean z_m) and a=mean sigmoid(z_m). A single route has constant 1/M influence on the pooled RAW score, whereas its probability-pool derivative is sigmoid'(z_m)/M and vanishes at saturation. Example with four members: z=(8,-1,-1,-1) gives g>1/2 and a<1/2 (exp(1)>2 suffices). Thus a high-magnitude minority can decide the logit pool while the probability pool follows the other members. Neither rule is universally preferable. With exactly TWO equally weighted binary members, their threshold-1/2 decisions agree because sigmoid(z1)+sigmoid(z2)>1 iff z1+z2>0; confidence and cross-candidate rankings can still differ.

Both pools have individual-loss comparison identities, not superiority over one another:

    mean CE(z_m,y)=CE(mean z_m,y)+A,
    A=mean logsumexp(z_m)-logsumexp(mean z_m)
     =mean KL(g||p_m)>=0;
    -log(a_y)<=mean[-log(p_my)].

The first is the saved Wood et al. ambiguity identity. More A can accompany worse members; it is not guaranteed accuracy/MRR gain. Individual calibration also does not automatically calibrate either pool.

Rank fusion instead discards score magnitude. Fixed Borda fusion assigns score minus the mean member rank within the SAME candidate list, with a fixed tie convention. It is invariant to each member's strictly increasing score transform; no calibration is needed. Raw mean scores are invariant to member-specific candidate-constant shifts but not positive scales. Member scales are implicit ensemble weights. Mean probabilities can change after BOTH shifts and scales because they change saturation. A common positive temperature AFTER raw pooling preserves binary ranking and multiclass argmax; improved NLL would not establish improved MRR/accuracy.

If every member orders every candidate pair identically, these positive monotone pools preserve that common order. Ranking gains need useful ordering differences, not confidence disagreement alone. Borda can lose useful calibrated margins; reciprocal-rank fusion is another rule with a parameter, not an automatically stronger or equivalent combiner.

2. Shared errors and graph regimes

For binary probability estimates let e_m=p_m-Y and Sigma=E[e e^T], including bias. Under squared loss, sum-one linear weights have risk w^T Sigma w. If Sigma is positive definite and negative weights are permitted, the oracle minimizer is

    w*=Sigma^(-1)1 / (1^T Sigma^(-1)1).

This requires ERROR second moments, not covariance of logits or hidden states. With centered covariance C and error bias b, Sigma=C+bb^T. Negative weights may produce invalid probabilities; simplex constraints change the solution. Estimated weights, classification CE and MRR inherit no oracle guarantee.

If e_m=u+v_m with shared u orthogonal to the private v vector, Sigma=tau^2 11^T+D. Every sum-one pool retains shared error u. With independent equal private variances sigma^2, uniform-pool MSE is tau^2+sigma^2/M. More members or covariance weighting cannot remove that common component under these assumptions.

Conditional error moments can differ by TRAIN-defined degree, relation type, feature/structure regime or endpoint context, so global weights can conceal opposing local behavior. This motivates attributed regime-dependent aggregation, not assuming low/high degree or heterophily predicts a particular winner. Learned routing is established prior. Correlated nodes/endpoints invalidate treating every prediction as an independent replication. BCE/calibration on sampled LP negatives concerns that artificial mixture, not the population probability of an edge. Per-query rank fusion fits HeaRT/citation candidate lists; shared-negative Hits needs its exact common score universe and evaluator, rather than silently converting to per-query ranking.

3. Hidden-state aggregation

A nonlinear decoder generally gives f(mean h_m) != mean f(h_m); equality holds for an affine common decoder. Concatenation, attention or learned weighting can change the readout class. Private states may have different coordinate gauges, so hidden-vector agreement/covariance need not mean prediction agreement or useful uncertainty. A capable single readout receiving the same complete bank can represent its deterministic pool. Learned fusion can improve finite learning, but capacity/calibration does not guarantee task quality. All-member dense evaluation still pays every route; a late gate provides no sparse-compute saving.
