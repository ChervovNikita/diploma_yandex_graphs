The count-conditioned auxiliary is connected on Collab, but only1.36% of the native positive queries offer genuine subset choices on both sides. A graph with different neighbourhood structure is therefore a useful scientific stress test.

The official OGB documentation identifies ogbl-ddi as a4267-node drug-interaction benchmark with a protein-target split and Hits@20 evaluation. This application and split differ from temporal collaboration prediction. There are no supplied node attributes in the author DDI paths: NCNC uses learned node IDs, while PENCIL disables feature fusion. Those representation differences must remain explicit.

DDI is a prospective candidate, chosen without acquiring its split/score payloads. Its published whole-graph edge count suggests denser neighbourhoods; it does not establish the auxiliary's informative support or computational cost on TRAIN. The first meaningful observation must be a complete TRAIN structural-support census under the native query masking and candidate contract. Dense support could make exact count inference prohibitively costly, which must be measured and preserved as a result.
