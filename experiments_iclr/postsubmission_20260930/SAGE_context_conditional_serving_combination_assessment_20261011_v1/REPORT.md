# Conditional SAGE acquisition and graph serving assessment

## Decision

**No combination is currently admitted.** Keep the failed sparse kernel recipe closed. Retain one conditional hypothesis: if the complete context-positive study establishes useful acquisition under its own frozen raw-accuracy and confidence criteria, the existing fixed graph-reliability scorer may recover some of its acquired correct alternatives that native pooling loses. Context predictive outcomes have not been opened for this assessment. This is a prospective decision note, not a serving study, source change or launch authorization.

The kernel does alter errors that a convex member-weight scorer on BASE cannot reverse: a wrong rival outranks truth in every original member. That is a distinct error mechanism, but the recorded private repair specificity is small. Of 700 private repairs, 640 are also served by common or fixed union controls; 60 are served by neither. Private also harms 156 readouts where BASE, common and fixed are all correct. The complete comparison does not isolate a same-state causal effect of private ownership. Its lower mean-member quality and coverage explain why fewer pooling losses did not yield better final predictions.

Graph serving supplies a separate, measured pooling mechanism. The paired bootstrap comparison is the closest tested combination ancestor: its native acquisition opens 127 original strict-rival cases to a correct member, then graph serving serves 50. It corrects zero remaining bootstrap strict rivals. All 11 serving-ingredient repairs and 13 of 18 acquisition-ingredient repairs survive their combination; 61 further baseline errors are repaired. Positive interaction coexists with worse final quality than equally processed capable references.

The released JSON does not join kernel repairs with graph-serving repairs on the same nodes. In particular, the overlap of kernel's 458 acquisitions or 260 original strict-rival corrections with graph's 281 repairs is unknown. Do not infer a transferable rescue fraction from these aggregates or select a prediction mixture from the repaired subsets.

## One conditional hypothesis

**A context procedure with useful acquired predictions, followed by fixed graph serving, can preserve its acquisition repairs and recover enough remaining pooling losses to improve final protected predictions.** This is an attributed reuse of the completed acquisition/serving mechanism. It carries no primitive-novelty claim.

The possible benefit is serving correct alternatives newly available in the acquired bank, especially cases that were strict common rivals in BASE. The expected harm is replacing already correct decisions with incorrect weighted decisions and retaining any member/coverage damage introduced by acquisition. Existing serving cannot reverse a common strict rival in its current input bank. The kernel evidence supplies no reason to reopen the kernel as an earned ingredient.

Context eligibility must be decided from its complete released results and existing five raw gates: +0.2 pp versus unchanged shared and equipped genuine I4; +0.1 pp versus pair-mixture masked, private full and shuffled masked; every seed nonnegative and at least two positive. Its calibrated NLL limits remain +0.02 mean and +0.05 at every seed. Member competence and coverage remain explanatory diagnostics, without an added numerical veto. The equipped singles and stronger original singles/I4 retain their reference limits. Useful acquisition and remaining pooling losses must be visible in the complete diagnostics; a repaired subset alone supplies no continuation admission.

If root admits this hypothesis, the common four cells are C = original shared native, A = admitted context procedure native, B = original shared graph, and A+B = admitted context procedure graph. The identical fixed graph/self scorers must also process every context control, equipped single/I4 and original capable reference. Reuse old serving endpoints only after exact bank, support, fold and operator identity is established. Freeze any new combination accuracy/confidence decision before opening its serving outcomes; this note does not revise the context study's decision or declare a new endpoint successful.

The decisive readout is same-node repair survival, new harms, acquisitions served/lost, previously served alternatives lost, fixed BASE strict-rival repairs, current-bank strict-rival corrections, member quality, final pooled accuracy and protected NLL. Report A, B and A+B versus C and A+B versus both ingredients and equal-processed capable references. Refute useful combination utility if serving harms offset its recovered alternatives, acquisition repairs disappear, or final protected quality fails its prospectively fixed references. An interaction statistic cannot rescue poorer final quality. A positive exploratory result takes unused whole-pipeline confirmation priority.

## Conditional cost and current boundary

No new backbone optimization is proposed. Reusing the complete 36-bank context roster would require fixed serving exports if its existing archives lack the graph scorer's required inputs. The 15 fresh four-member banks imply 75 graph-head fits (five folds per bank) plus 75 matched self-head fits; one-member convex serving is degenerate. Old matched endpoints could be reused. Historical shared SAGE graph fitting took about 9 seconds per bank for five folds, so the graph/self work is of the order of a few CPU minutes, with export/forward costs separate and unmeasured here. This is a workload estimate, not an inference benchmark or an approved job.

Current action: hand this evidence and conditional decision to root; await complete context interpretation. The sealed reader, production freeze, canonical state and original outcomes are preserved. No arrays, models, checkpoint state, TEST payloads, remote operations, new fits or fallback grids were used.
