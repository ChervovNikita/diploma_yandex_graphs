Training-Free Cross-Architecture Merging for Graph Neural Networks
Rishabh Bhattacharya * 1 Vikaskumar Kalsariya * 1 Naresh Manwani 1
Abstract
Model merging has emerged as a powerful
paradigm for combining the capabilities of dis-
tinct expert models without the high computa-
tional cost of retraining, yet current methods are
fundamentally constrained tohomogeneous ar-
chitectures. For GNNs, however, message pass-
ing istopology-dependentand sensitive to mis-
alignment, making direct parameter-space merg-
ing unreliable. To bridge this gap, we introduce
H-GRAMA(Heterogeneous Graph Routing and
Message Alignment), a training-free framework
that lifts merging fromparameter spacetoop-
erator space. We formalizeUniversal Message
Passing Mixture (UMPM), a shared operator
family that expresses heterogeneous GNN layers
in a common functional language. H-GRAMA
enablescross-architecture GNN merging(e.g.,
GCN↔GAT) without retraining, retaining high
specialist accuracy in most cases in compatible
depth settings and achieving inference speedups
of1.2×–1.9×over ensembles.
1. Introduction
The graph domain is beginning to surface its own merging-
specific obstacles. Deep Graph Mating frames learning-free
reuse for GNNs and highlights the sensitivity of interpo-
lation to topology-dependent effects, proposing message
coordination and message-statistics calibration as key in-
gredients (Jing et al., 2024). More recently, Garg et al.
(2025) benchmark model merging for GNNs and propose
embedding alignment to improve practical performance.
These works substantiate that GNN merging isnota direct
transplant of vision/NLP merging recipes, and that control-
ling themessage distributionis central. However, existing
formulations typically assume homogeneous architectures
or address a different reuse goal than ourspecialist node-

We study a concrete and practically motivated composition
problem insingle-graph transductive node classification.
We are provided two pretrainedspecialistparent GNNs
trained underdifferent labeled node subsets(training masks)
on thesamegraph. Following the split-data protocol of
Ainsworth et al. (2023), we partition the training nodes into
two disjoint subsets using a fixedspecialist class split(used
only during parent training and evaluation): subset A con-
tains 80% of labeled training nodes from one class group
and 20% from the other, while subset B uses the complemen-
tary allocation. Parent fA is trained on subset A and Parent
fB on subset B, while both models predict over the same
label space {0, . . . , K−1} . The parents may beheteroge-
neous(e.g., GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017), GAT (Veliˇckovi´c
et al., 2018), GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., 2017), GIN (Xu
et al., 2019)), and may differ in depth and width. Our goal is
to construct asinglechild model fC that preserves each par-
ent’s performance on its own specialist node subset, while
running as one network at inference time andwithoutany
gradient-based training, distillation, or fine-tuning during
the merge.
At first glance, this objective resembles distillation,
mixtures-of-experts, continual learning, or federated ag-
gregation, but these paradigms typically require additional
optimization, multi-expert inference, or architectural com-
patibility. Our setting instead requires asinglemerged GNN
producedtraining-freeandlabel-freeat merge time, while
handling heterogeneous GNN operators and depth/width
mismatch.

pendently fine-tuned checkpoints that share a pretrained
initialization (Wortsman et al., 2022a). However, in the
specialist regime and with heterogeneous GNN backbones,
naive parameter mixing is brittle: the same function can be
