we want to utilize a fine-tuned PiSSA, similar to initializing one, we first perform singular value decomposition on the

4 Experiments
We conduct comparative experiments between PiSSA, LoRA, and full parameter fine-tuning on LLaMA 2-7B [48],
Mistral-7B-v0.1 [49], and Gemma-7B [50]. These comparing cover a wide range of tasks, including math [2, 51, 52],
coding [4, 53, 54, 55], and instruction tuning [5, 7, 6]. The findings reveal that fine-tuning adapters initialized with
principal singular values and vectors (PiSSA) outperform those initialized with intermediate or less significant ones.
This evidences that direct fine-tuning of the model’s principal components leads to superior outcomes (Section 4.1).
PiSSA demonstrates superior performance on downstream tasks (Section 4.3). Subsequently, we conduct experiments
across various ranks, observing that PiSSA converges more swiftly and aligns more closely with the training data
compared to LoRA, exhibiting robust superiority under the same trainable parameter configurations (Section 4.2).
Finally, we employ subsequent experiments to demonstrate that the Fast SVD [56] technique helps our PiSSA achieve a
balance between initialization speed and performance (Section 4.4).
In our experiments, we adopt the Alpaca [57] implementation strategy, using the AdamW optimizer with a batch size of
128, a learning rate of 2e-5, cosine annealing schedules, and a warmup ratio of 0.03, without any weight decay. We
utilize the bf16 computation type, ensure lora_alpha is always equal to lora_r, set lora_dropout to 0, and incorporate the
adapters into all linear layers of the base model. We train only on the first 100,000 conversions from the dataset for one
epoch, and only using the response of the conversions to compute loss. The experiments in this section were conducted
on the NVIDIA A800-SXM4(80G) GPU.
