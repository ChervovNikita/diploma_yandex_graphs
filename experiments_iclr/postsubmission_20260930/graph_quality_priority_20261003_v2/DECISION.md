## Distinct retained direction during training

A shared update can erode a correct member's relative logit margin while member loss gradients remain positively aligned. This is a mathematical example, not a measured property of our current models. A proposed correction protects selected margin responses while retaining the native pooled-loss directional progress.

The closest operator check identifies GEM as a direct prior. At a frozen reference, negative protected margins are virtual losses, and their gradient inequalities reproduce GEM's Euclidean projection constraints. Adding pooled-progress equality does not justify claiming a new projection principle. The scientific question is whether graph-dependent selection of useful responses improves later predictions.
