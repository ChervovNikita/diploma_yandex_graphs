# From the literature to a quality-oriented GNNM extension

5 October 2026. Research decision, not a manuscript contribution claim.

## Objective and present evidence

The objective is a learnable shared-backbone ensemble with better predictions than both a competent single model and an ordinary independently trained ensemble. Parameter savings are secondary. The completed five-seed Collab comparison improves on single64 by 0.8483 percentage points, but independent4 remains 0.3389 points ahead. This does not satisfy the objective. Its TEST predictions are already consumed; their decision-overlap analysis is exploratory.

The literature is being used to compare actual operations, training objectives and served predictions. The canonical index contains scoped conclusions, including incomplete reads. It is not a claim that every listed paper has been read in full or that novelty has been exhaustively cleared.

## Scientific constraint on the goal

An unrestricted ensemble of the same constituent networks can represent a tied ensemble by choosing equal shared weights. Weight sharing therefore supplies no general function-class superiority over independent networks. A quality advantage would have to come from useful inductive bias, finite-sample estimation or optimization, possibly together with a different graph-specific operation. A larger independent comparator given that same operation remains essential. More distant embeddings or different graph masks do not prove better predictions.

## Main predictive hypothesis being tested

For a candidate link between two nodes, a graph-completion model considers missing connections around both endpoints. Averaging completion probabilities separately can combine one member's explanation at the left endpoint with another member's incompatible explanation at the right. The joint auxiliary instead assigns one member to the complete two-sided restoration pattern, while retaining the same observed graph context and supervision.

This is a graph-specific application of established mixture mathematics. The current separate-endpoint control preserves whole-side mixture distributions at a fixed model state. Separately trained models can change those distributions, so an observed quality difference would measure the training intervention rather than a guaranteed pure dependence effect. Training-mask association can also fail to transfer to future links. Saved theory already provides such failure cases.

Serving remains the declared native ensemble pool. There is no new learned serving gate. If the auxiliary improves reconstruction while target ranking ties or declines, the quality hypothesis fails at this setting. A gain over a single alone is insufficient. A positive development screen still needs matched quality controls and prospective confirmation; the original submitted scores remain unchanged.
