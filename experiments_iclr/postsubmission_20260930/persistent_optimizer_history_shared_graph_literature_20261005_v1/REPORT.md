## Decision

The new scopes establish relevant ancestry for **carried optimization accumulators** and **carried graph-encoder history**, but neither establishes the active conjunction: shared graph features trained through a current private Adam response, persistent route-specific parameters and moments, endpoint-conditioned link supervision, recomputed private commitment, and fixed mean-logit serving.

There is no supported successor or new graph-history control ready for adoption. The one unresolved comparison worth retaining is the already saved question about alignment of each private row with its own complete Adam history. It remains a conceptual falsifier, with substantial interpretation limits; it adds no arm or gate. These checked scopes do not support a global absence or novelty claim.

## How history enters our current derivative

Let a private state be `s_j = (u_j, m_j, v_j, t_j)` and its current inner response be `s'_j = T(theta, I_j, s_j)`. The active outer derivative trains shared `theta` through the current response while treating old `s_j` as fixed. The old moments still affect that response: momentum, adaptive scaling, bias correction and the parameter step depend on them. Carrying state is therefore substantive even though earlier learning is outside the derivative graph.

After the shared update, the private step is recomputed from the unchanged old state at the new shared parameters. This provides a same-episode commitment interpretation. It does not differentiate the complete historical trajectory or assign credit to how earlier moments were formed. A favorable live-versus-detached result would support current-response credit conditional on carried state; it would not isolate the benefit of retention or its alignment with private weights.

History may smooth noisy gradients, supply adaptive preconditioning or become stale as shared features move. All are ordinary optimization explanations. Current endpoint exclusion also leaves earlier label exposure in private parameters and moments. It restricts today's inner labels, rather than removing historical supervision or creating independent tasks. Neither new paper supplies a guarantee for native Adam, graph ranking, the half pooled/half member BCE objective, or predictive superiority.

The saved index already establishes MetaReg's persistent source heads and differentiable virtual private updates, MLDG's training-only transfer objective, and graph update/recomputation ancestry from AUX-TS and related scopes. It also records uncertainty about optimizer-state semantics in ABMLL, TMAG and other graph meta methods. The new papers refine the distinction between optimizer accumulators, EMA teacher state and current-response differentiation; they do not erase that ancestry or close those implementation uncertainties.

## The sole retained unresolved comparison

**Question:** once distinct private histories exist, does matching each current private row with its own complete old Adam `m/v/step` state add useful predictive behavior, compared with a cyclic reassignment of those complete histories among identically shaped rows while current weights, shared weights, queries, supports, masks and learning rates are held fixed?

This is the existing saved alignment falsifier, not a newly prepared experimental arm. It targets a narrower claim than “memory helps” and cannot be answered by fixed30 live/detached controls or descriptive F4–F1 companions. Resetting history instead would simultaneously change warm-up, bias correction and adaptive scale, so it would not answer the same alignment question.

It is **not ready for adoption**. Factor/normalization/beta coordinates and any row symmetries must first have an unambiguous compatible mapping. Swapped histories deliberately break the relationship between a row's weights and its lawful past trajectory. Harm could therefore reflect generic Adam mismatch or coordinate misalignment, without demonstrating graph-specific memory, differentiated-history credit or ensemble necessity. A changed response alone is insufficient; the relevant claim concerns a prospectively specified predictive contrast. The two new scopes supply no validated graph-LP protocol or result for that intervention. No comparison is prepared, launched or added to the frozen studies here.
