Abstract
In this paper, we propose FAGEL, an efficient ensemble framework for large-scale graph learning based on batch sampling. Our approach introduces two key components: staged training and structured sampling diversification. FAGEL first trains a GNN using a fixed subgraph sampling strategy until performance plateaus based on early stopping criteria. Rather than terminating the process, FAGEL continues a second stage training based on sampling configurations with varied hop depths and neighbor sizes, enabling the model to capture complementary structural patterns across diverse receptive fields. During training, early stopping is used to monitor validation performance, and the best-performing models are saved throughout the optimization process, producing a set of diverse yet efficiently trained models within a single training pipeline. To integrate these models, FAGEL adopts a residual weighted ensemble strategy that adaptively aggregates their predictions, leveraging structural diversity induced by diversified sampling. Our theoretical study asserts that changing sampling configurations helps improve embedding diversity, compared to the stochastic variation produced by repeated sampling under a fixed configuration, providing complementary and diverse models. Empirical results on various benchmark graphs, ranging from 3,000 nodes to million node scales, demonstrate significant performance gains, in both speed and accuracy, compared to baseline approaches. Notably, FAGEL enables training ensemble GNN models for million-scale graphs using a single GPU with only 32 GB of memory, highlighting the practical scalability and efficiency of the proposed framework. Code and benchmark datasets are available at https://github.com/Chrisshen12/FAGEL.
