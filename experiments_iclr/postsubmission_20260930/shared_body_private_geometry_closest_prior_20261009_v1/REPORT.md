# Closest prior for shared-body ensembles with private graph geometry

## Bounded conclusion

**Shared network weights with learned, private sheaf geometry and predictive ensembling already have a primary precedent: Bayesian Sheaf Neural Networks (BSNN).** Its §3 learns an endpoint-conditioned distribution over restriction maps, jointly trains that learner and one network, and ensembles predictions over sampled sheaves. Persistent deterministic members with private BatchEnsemble factors have different parameter and training semantics, but “sharing weights while ensembling private geometry” is an unsafe novelty claim.

Two additional collisions matter. **Bundle Neural Networks (BuNN)** explicitly process several learned bundles in parallel and mix their channels in one model. **HetSheaf-ensemble** explicitly supplies several edge-type-specific restriction-map predictors over common projected features. These are strong controls for multiple geometries and separate geometry generators. Their inspected methods do not implement a persistent probability-averaged committee of members traversing the entire same graph.

The strongest remaining hypothesis concerns **coherent graph-wide connection modes under weight sharing**, rather than the existence of private geometry. BSNN explicitly factorizes its variational distribution over incidences. Persistent global factors could couple a member's maps across many edges and, through its states, across layers. Whether this improves useful predictive specialization at a measured budget is untested. A structured BSNN or a joint multi-transport single model could account for the entire effect. No new objective, superiority, Bayesian validity, or substantive method novelty is established here.

## 1. Target and collision test

The inspected source design prepares complete member paths of original licensed NSD. Shared native weight/bias/epsilon objects are combined with member-private input/output factors on the input lift, feature maps, incidence learner and classifier. The native incidence learner consumes the ordered current endpoint states and produces general matrix restriction maps. Each member retains its own states, maps, normalization and propagation. Training is the mean of member TRAIN NLLs; serving averages probabilities. All members therefore execute graph work. Stored weight sharing supplies no single shared feature trajectory or automatic speedup.

A direct collision should be checked along five axes: shared learned feature/network weights; distinct complete graph operators; endpoint-conditioned matrix maps; joint task training; and predictive member pooling. A paper using “ensemble,” multiple attention heads, multiple edge types, or multiple layers does not automatically meet all five axes.

## 2. BSNN: what differs, and what limitation is actually inspected

The relevant distinction is between a **distribution of geometry** and a **finite collection of persistent learned functions**.

| Axis | Inspected BSNN §3 | Persistent shared/private NSD design | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Geometry ownership | A learned conditional distribution `q_phi(F | X,y)`; §3.2 factorizes it over node-edge incidences. Parameters of each factor come from the ordered endpoint features. | A fixed collection of trainable member factors modulates shared incidence weights; each member computes a whole graph's maps from its current states. | A member's global factors can couple changes across many edges. This is a possible representation advantage over the *inspected factorized family*, not over every Bayesian sheaf model. |
| Layer/state conditioning | §3.3 constructs distributions from the input features and samples independent sheaves, one per layer. | Current member endpoint states can change maps at every layer. | State-conditioned structured BSNN and ordinary deterministic NSD are necessary controls for an apparent benefit of this difference. |
| Learned feature path | One set of network parameters θ accompanies sheaf samples; sampled geometry produces different hidden states. | Slow weights are shared, but private feature/head factors can change the effective feature path as well as geometry. | An improvement may come from private features or heads. Weight sharing does not imply identical features. |
| Training | One-sample MC training estimator of likelihood minus an annealed/weighted prior KL; joint gradients train θ and φ. | Mean of the persistent members' own supervised NLLs, with no sheaf posterior KL. | Removing KL or MC noise changes regularization/optimization. These must be separated from a geometry mechanism. |
| Serving | Ensembling over newly sampled sheaves for point prediction. | Saved members and restored checkpoints produce a finite mean of probabilities. | Persistent identities can make evaluation reproducible. They do not guarantee calibrated epistemic uncertainty or useful specialization. |

BSNN §3.2 explicitly says its incidence-product assumption restricts the variational family and makes KL computation tractable. This is a concrete limitation: conditional sampling lacks a stochastic global latent variable that jointly chooses a graph-wide connection mode across incidences. Shared φ still couples the *learned distribution parameters* through features and training; it does not remove the stated conditional factorization of sheaf draws. §3.3's input-conditioned, independent layer draws also differ from a persistent member's recurrently changing geometry.

A persistent member is a possible way to represent coherent modes, but a **structured BSNN with a global function-level latent variable** could represent the same coupling. Resampled global factors, low-rank variational factors, or a conditional sheaf generator could also do so. Persistent fast factors are not uniquely justified by the limitation. Treating learned deterministic members as posterior samples is unsupported; Dirac member locations are not automatically a finite-KL approximation to the continuous prior in the inspected ELBO.

For fixed persistent functions, the supervised objective is

`L_finite = mean_m [-log p_(theta,eta_m)(y | X)]`, with `p_pool = mean_m p_m`.

BSNN's negative ELBO instead contains an expected conditional NLL plus its prior KL. Both train individual geometry-conditioned predictions, so the mean-own-loss aspect is closely related. Training `-log mean_m p_m(y)` is a different pooled objective; Jensen's inequality relates its value to the mean-own loss, but their training derivatives and member responsibility differ. No generic repulsion term is needed to formulate this comparison.

## 3. Can private geometry escape a gauge interpretation?

**Yes, in a declared connection class, but matrices being different is insufficient.** Every geometry still has coordinate descriptions; the useful question is whether members are gauge-equivalent to one operator or to trivial scalar graph transport.

For an orthogonal connection transporting from u to v, a node-wise frame change gives

`x'_v = Q_v x_v`, `T'_(v<-u) = Q_v T_(v<-u) Q_u^T`.

If `T_(v<-u) = R_v^T R_u`, setting `z_v = R_v x_v` turns aggregation into ordinary graph aggregation in synchronized coordinates. On a closed loop these transports telescope to identity. BuNN §3 and the saved FlatNSD §3/Appendix C directly instantiate this factorization [U,F]. Multiple flat member bundles can therefore be several local frame fields around shared graph diffusion. Their nonlinear encoders/readouts may still make a useful model, but usefulness alone does not establish irreducible private connection geometry.

For a genuine orthogonal connection, a loop product transforms by conjugation at the starting node. A nonidentity loop product cannot be removed by node frames to yield the trivial connection. Differences in appropriate loop conjugacy invariants between members can witness gauge-inequivalent geometry. A global connection Laplacian spectrum is also invariant under orthogonal block conjugation. Individual loop spectra do not form a complete equivalence test: several loops must be compatible with a common frame change, and isospectral operators need not be gauge-equivalent. On a tree, invertible connection transports with consistent inverse reverse maps can be gauged to identity; cycles are essential to this particular obstruction.

The original **general NSD** maps are arbitrary matrices and can be singular. Products `F_v^T F_u` and normalized blocks are not automatically orthogonal connection transports; transpose need not mean inverse. Do not report generic loop products as geometric holonomy without these assumptions. Ranks, kernels, singular values under a declared orthogonal gauge, normalized-operator spectra, and consistently defined closed-walk products may supply algebraic diagnostics. Full GL(d) coordinate changes also require a consistently transformed inner product/adjoint/normalization; orthogonal-gauge arguments cannot simply be asserted for arbitrary invertible frames.

There is also a **whole-network** distinction. Keeping shared feature weights, ELU, normalization, bias, head and readout numerically fixed while changing only frames generally changes the represented function. Coordinatewise ELU is not equivariant to arbitrary rotations, and left stalk mixing/head coordinates need corresponding changes for a true reparameterization. A flat transport can consequently improve prediction through feature-frame alignment. That is a plausible node-adapter mechanism, rather than evidence for curvature or a topological ensemble principle.

HetSheaf's §4 and Appendix A.1 independently identify the readout/gauge problem. Its whitening plus anchor/Procrustes alignment is a published approach; Appendix A.1 explicitly describes finite-sample/conditioning limitations and approximate invariance [H]. We do not promote its GL(d) robustness claim into an unconditional exact theorem.

For this source, analyze the **actual evaluation operator** with its true normalization, degree terms, clamping and residual placement. The native saved `.L` diagnostic is an unnormalized detached transport, as the reused source review notes. Neither raw map distances nor differences in this diagnostic establish diversity of deployed operators. Training jitter and coordinate-specific regularization must also be separated from an evaluation-time gauge claim.

## 4. One worthwhile, falsifiable hypothesis

> At a fixed measured budget, persistent global corrections to a shared endpoint-conditioned sheaf learner may capture coherent, task-relevant graph-wide operator modes that an incidence-factorized BSNN represents inefficiently. Small private feature corrections may let a shared weight bank support those modes without requiring independent full encoders. Any benefit should survive node-frame/capacity controls, occur in the actual geometry-conditioned predictive functions, and improve a restored finite committee rather than merely increase disagreement.

This is a **mechanism and sharing-placement hypothesis within established ingredients**. It asserts no advantage, posterior interpretation, new loss or novelty. Learned persistence may also overfit, collapse to one member, encode only local coordinate adapters, or save stored parameters while increasing total graph work.

## 5. What would support superiority or novelty?

A **superiority claim** would need a repeatable quality/cost frontier against competent BSNN, independent NSD and joint multi-transport controls, complete validation selection and restored serving states, and prospective confirmation across tasks/seeds. Account for M state trajectories, edge-map generation, degree/SVD normalization, sparse multiplication, backwards, optimizer state, persistent diagnostics, serving draws, peak memory and wall time. Parameter count is one budget, not the entire resource result.

A **mechanism claim** would additionally need transport/feature/head ownership controls and gauge-aware evidence that the useful differences are coherent graph operators rather than extra node/head capacity or coordinate alignment. A **novelty claim** needs a distinct learning operation, an assumption-qualified separation/guarantee, or a scientifically new demonstrated sharing/geometry phenomenon after the close BSNN/BuNN/HetSheaf comparisons. A renamed finite ensemble, altered number of samples, routine BatchEnsemble application to NSD, or raw map disagreement is insufficient. Strong empirical evidence could justify a limited efficient sharing/placement result without asserting a new sheaf principle.
