## Owen2007, The pigeonhole bootstrap

DOI10.1214/07-AOAS122, Annals of Applied Statistics. The abstract explicitly
connects crossed, severely unbalanced random effects to recommender systems and
large bipartite weighted graphs. It warns that naive bootstrap sampling can be
misleading and describes separate row/column resampling with a mean-consistency
result under its stated model class. That is ancestry for respecting endpoint
dependence, not a theorem for shared-backbone ensemble quality.

## Owen and Eckles2012, Bootstrapping data arrays of arbitrary order

DOI10.1214/12-AOAS547, Annals of Applied Statistics. The abstract describes
independently reweighting factor levels and giving observations the PRODUCT of
factor weights. Its mildly conservative variance-estimation statement needs
sufficient conditions; it is not unbiased finite-sample inference and does not
transfer to our task loss, private gradients, or within-batch normalization.

## Decision for the current link candidate

Our prepared v2 responsibility law is an ADDITIVE, positive endpoint field.
It differs from the product law described by Owen/Eckles. Its incidence-matrix
raw covariance calculation is a property of that declared construction, not a
new bootstrap principle. Its purpose is to test useful persistent route credit
with a shared GNN, not to estimate a data-sampling variance or posterior.
Group-mean normalization and dependence caveats remain explicit. Do not describe
endpoint-correlated weights as a newly discovered idea or claim these papers
justify confidence intervals for graph queries. A product-weight comparator
would require a separate prospectively specified law/control and actual native
integration; it is not silently inserted into the benchmark or counted now.
