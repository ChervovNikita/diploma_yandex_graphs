4. Reading magnitude and direction together

For each shared bank/group:
  cosine(i,j) = dot(g_i,g_j)/(||g_i|| ||g_j||), if both norms are nonzero.
  ratio = ||mean_m g_m||^2 / mean_m ||g_m||^2, if the denominator is nonzero.
  Equivalently, ratio = (sum_m ||g_m||^2 + 2 sum_{i<j} dot(g_i,g_j))
                       / (4 sum_m ||g_m||^2).

Small absolute gradients are not evidence of conflict. A negative cosine
describes opposing directions, while its dot and the route norms show how much
energy those directions contribute. A small ratio can also reflect unequal
route magnitudes or zero routes even when the defined pair cosines are positive.
Read all three together; do not introduce a post hoc conflict threshold.

Report whole shared-body/native gradients and conditional-builder groups
separately. An effect confined to one builder group cannot describe every
shared native parameter. Norm comparisons must carry dimensions and actual
parameter roles; fitted scales and parameterization differ between groups and
models. Across banks/seeds, compare scalar diagnostics descriptively only.

Private r/s rows belong to different registered coordinates. Their separate
active-row norms describe own-loss sensitivity. Do not report their disjoint
ownership as useful orthogonality, diversity or a cancellation advantage.
Identical group names in M1/I4 do not align independently learned bodies; no
cross-body cosine, direction average or cancellation ratio is meaningful here.
