## Suggested horizon range

Consider **60–180 complete outer cycles** for the first representative pilot, with one common maximum chosen before outcomes after all architecture/rule costs are known. Sixty cycles is the shortest useful lower bound under the existing complete-VALID-every-five-cycles and eleven-miss rule: an initial best at cycle 5 needs eleven subsequent checks through cycle 60 to establish even that minimal plateau. Sixty is a bounded exploration horizon, not evidence that a late-improving model is trained adequately. The upper portion, around 120–180, allows 24–36 maximum validation checks and room for later improvement. A shorter 5–20-cycle run can estimate setup or detect gross failure, but should not be presented as a decisive model comparison.

The verified candidate first complete cycle took **193.7213 seconds**. Straight multiplication gives approximately 3.23 hours for 60 cycles, 6.46 hours for 120, or 9.69 hours for 180, **per candidate fit**. These are planning estimates from one cycle, excluding additional validation/startup variation and any early stop. Other control costs are pending; do not price the entire family using this candidate rate. The original supervisor failed on an exit race, while the complete scientific cycle was recovered and independently verified. The receipt admits cost estimation only, not quality or an unfailed supervisor.

There is no reason to favor an easily trained reference. Keep the capable single's entire nonlinear predictor adaptable and the ordinary native ensemble's independent full encoders/heads. If any important reference or candidate is still materially improving at the fixed cap, the pilot is horizon-limited: report that and use a separately frozen continuation if worthwhile. Do not interpret an undertrained reference as a method win, shorten its horizon based on cost, or quietly extend only a promising candidate.
