201: 3.2 Complementary Feature Masking
202: During initial experiments, we observe that simply providing estimated depth and RGB images to the network does not enable the network to leverage the full potential of all provided information.
203: We refer the reader to Sec. 4.2 for details of that analysis.
204: We hypothesize that the network grows too confident in the RGB encoder and thus dismisses complementary information from the depth encoder, which limits adaptability to the target domain.
205: To improve cross-modal information exchange, we therefore introduce a cross-modal masking strategy.
206: In contrast to Masked Image Modeling in UDA [23], our method involves masking the learned representation of different modalities on feature-level rather than a single modality on input-level.
207: Moreover, our masking strategy and schedule are specifically designed to improve redundancy across modalities and prevent getting stuck in local minima due to one encoder already being pretrained.
208: Complementary Dropout. For this, we propose using blockwise dropout [9] to generate masked features.
209: When masking only individual pixels, the information could be easily restored from the neighborhood in the same modality without requiring the other modality.
210: When masking larger blocks, the network has to understand the semantics of the other modality to recover the missing information.
211: We, therefore, opt to mask out whole blocks within the feature map according to a predefined schedule.
212: Furthermore, we hypothesize that learning complementary features across modalities can be achieved best by masking the feature maps of different modalities in complementary fashion.
213: For example, if we mask 70% of the RGB features, we would mask the remaining 30% in the depth features.
214: This intuitively corrupts the information across modalities and forces the network to rely on features from all modalities.

222: To fulfill our goal of true cross-modal complementary masking, we use the same masking across all feature map levels and experimentally validate that design in Sec. 4.2.
223: Conceptually, this avoids the recovery of features within the feature pyramid of the same modality.
224: Therefore, our method is designed to foster the transfer of complementary information and to promote the learning of potentially redundant information, which in turn increases robustness and reduces sensitivity to domain-specific appearance changes.
225: Masking Schedule.
226: Prior studies [9, 30] have highlighted the limitations of a static masking ratio.
227: In response, we adopt a dynamic masking ratio schedule for RGB and depth features.
