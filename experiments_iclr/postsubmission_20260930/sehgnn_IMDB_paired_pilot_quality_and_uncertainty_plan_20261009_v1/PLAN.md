## Paired uncertainty and multiplicity

For both primary and all five shared-control contrasts, show all three paired differences, their equal-weight mean, standard deviation, range and leave-one-pair-out means. The role and optimizer changes are coupled; their effects cannot be separated. Validation roles may overlap, labels within a movie are dependent, and all repetitions share one graph. The effective evidence is three paired study repetitions, not twelve members, fifteen label scores or thousands of independent events.

If a paired mean interval is reported, identify its assumptions: a conventional t interval has two degrees of freedom and assumes independent repetitions. Treat it as a fragile conditional summary, not a graph-generalization interval. Node/event resampling does not remove graph dependence or VALID selection optimism. A simple two-sided sign-flip test has only eight sign allocations at three pairs and a minimum attainable p-value of 0.25; the pilot cannot deliver ordinary 0.05 evidence this way.

The two genuine-reference comparisons are joint primary requirements, not a menu from which to choose a favorable comparator. Five shared contrasts, label/stratum results and U/D entries add multiplicity. Present the complete families; do not promote the smallest p-value or favorable cell. Formal confirmation must freeze its claim families and error control, such as Holm adjustment where applicable, before outcomes. Failure to detect a difference is not equivalence. An equivalence or noninferiority claim needs its own practical margin, design and power.
