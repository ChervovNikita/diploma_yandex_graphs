## Sections3.1-3.2
3.1 Graph Generation with Persistent Homology
Figure 2: Graph generation using persistent homology. Each graph is generated by the filtration at the threshold [MATH \epsilon_{t}] . The graphs are fed separately into arbitrary functions [MATH f_{\theta_{k}}] parametrized by learnable weights [MATH \theta_{k}] (e.g., neural networks), which are then aggregated ( [MATH AGG] ) to obtain the representations [MATH \mathbf{\overline{Z}}] for the downstream tasks.
The “death times” in the filtration mark critical thresholds where the data’s structure undergoes topological changes. To leverage this, we construct a multi-scale representation of the data as a series of discrete graphs, where each captures the topology at a specific scale defined by its death times. To integrate this representation with GNNs, we extract the “skeleton” of the simplicial complexes by considering only pairwise node connections. Algorithm 1 details this process. We generate three graph sets: [MATH G^{0}] having graphs whose simplicial complexes contain only [MATH H_{0}] ; [MATH G^{1}] having graphs whose simplicial complexes contain both [MATH H_{0}] and [MATH H_{1}] ; and [MATH G^{0,1}] being the union of [MATH G^{0}] and [MATH G^{1}] . For instance, in Figure 2, graphs at [MATH \epsilon_{1}] and [MATH \epsilon_{2}] belong to [MATH G^{0}] , while the graph at [MATH \epsilon_{K}] is part of [MATH G^{1}] .
More formally, given a dataset [MATH X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}] and its associated persistent homology, we construct graphs based on the Vietoris-Rips filtration [MATH C=\{C_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}] . For each [MATH d] -dimensional topological feature, such as connected components for [MATH d=0] or loops for [MATH d=1] , we identify the set of death times [MATH \{\tau_{i}^{d}\}_{i=1}^{b_{d}}] , where [MATH b_{d}] denotes the total number of these features in dimension [MATH d] . At each death time [MATH \tau_{i}^{d}] , we define a graph [MATH G_{i}^{d}=(V,E_{i}^{d})] , with [MATH V=X] as the vertex set and [MATH E_{i}^{d}] containing edges between points [MATH x_{\alpha}] and [MATH x_{\beta}] if their pairwise distance satisfies [MATH \|x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta}\|\leq\tau_{i}^{d}] . The resulting collection of graphs, [MATH \mathcal{G}^{d}=\{G_{i}^{d}\}_{i=1}^{b_{d}}] , represents the data’s topological features across multiple scales, capturing relationships from fine-grained local structures to global patterns.
3.2 Model Architecture: Ensembles of GNNs
We design ensembles of neural networks to encode the PH-induced graphs, where each sub-network takes a distinct graph input. The general architecture of the [MATH i-] th sub-network processing the [MATH i-] th graph with the respective adjacency matrix [MATH \mathbf{A}^{(i)}] is defined as:
[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{(i)}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\left(f^{(i)}\left(\mathbf{D};\mathbf{\Theta}^{(i)}\right)\right),]

[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{(i)}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\sigma_{2}\left(g^{(i)}\left(\mathbf{X}^{(i)},\mathbf{A}^{(i)};\mathbf{\Psi}^{(i)}\right)\right),]
(7)
[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{\overline{Z}}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i}\mathbf{Z}^{(i)},]

[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{\overline{Y}}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\text{MLP}(\mathbf{\overline{Z}};\mathbf{\Phi}),]
(9)
where [MATH \sigma_{1}] and [MATH \sigma_{2}] are non-linear activation functions, and [MATH f_{i}(.)] is an arbitrary function chosen depending on the applications to extract the temporal features from the time series. The function [MATH g_{i}(.)] is the 2-layer GCN model where each message-passing layer is defined as in Eq 3. The encodings [MATH \mathbf{H}^{(i)}] are set to be the node representations of the GCN’s last layer. The matrices [MATH \mathbf{\Theta}^{(i)},\mathbf{\Psi}^{(i)}] are learnable weights of [MATH f^{(i)}] and [MATH g^{(i)}] , and [MATH \mathbf{\Phi}] is learnable weights shared across subnetworks. The operator [MATH \bigoplus] defines an aggregator to fuse the output to obtain the final representation for the downstream task. Here, we adapt the attention-based mechanism Bahdanau et al. (2015) to guide the model to select the most important graphs using the outputs of the GCNs as weights:
[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{(i)}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{(i)}\mathbf{W}_{att},]
(10)
[MATH \displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i)}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\mathbf{S}^{(i)})}{\sum_{j}\exp(\mathbf{S}^{(j)})},]
(11)
[MATH \displaystyle\mathbf{\overline{Z}}]
[MATH \displaystyle=\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i)}\odot\mathbf{Z}^{(i)}.,]
(12)
where [MATH \mathbf{W}_{att}] is trainable weights shared across sub-networks, and [MATH \odot] denotes the Hadamard product. The representations are passed through a multilayer perceptron (MLP) to adapt to output dimensions for the node-level regression tasks.

## Sections3.4-3.5
3.4 Earthquake Predictions
Time-series Extrinsic Regression (TSER) tasks study the relationship between the entire time series sequence and external variables Tan et al. (2021). The objective of earthquake predictions is to regress five seismic variables, known as maximum intensity measurements (IMs) for each seismic sensor given their multivariate time series input.
Recent work Bloemheuvel et al. (2023) has shown that GNNs have proven effective by enabling sensors to exchange information, improving the accuracy of IM predictions even for distant sensors. To demonstrate the effectiveness of PH-induced graphs, we keep the architecture design’s effort minimal, with each sub-network in the ensemble following a similar architecture as in their work. In particular, [MATH f^{(i)}] is a 2-layer 1D convolutional layers Kiranyaz et al. (2021) to extract the temporal features, with [MATH \sigma_{1}(.)] as the ReLU function. The output of the GCN’s first layer passes through a ReLU function, while the final layer’s output is processed using a Tanh function, i.e., [MATH \text{Tanh}(x)=\frac{\exp^{x}-\exp^{-x}}{\exp^{x}+\exp^{-x}}] . The output is then fed into 5 separate MLPs, where each predicts one IM value.
The time series tensor is defined as [MATH \mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times N\times W\times C}] , where [MATH T] denotes the number of earthquakes, [MATH W] is the input length of the time series, [MATH N] is the number of sensors, and [MATH C] is the number of channels. The objective is to minimize a mean-square error (MSE) loss:
[MATH \mathcal{L}_{tser}=\frac{1}{N}\|\overline{\mathbf{Y}}-\mathbf{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|\mathbf{W}\|_{2}^{2}]
(21)
where [MATH \mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 5}] are the labels, and the second term is an L2 regularizer, with [MATH \mathbf{W}] being the entire model’s weights and [MATH \lambda=10^{-4}] controlling the regularization strength.
3.5 Traffic Forecasting
We study the traffic speed forecasting task using historical data of a network of traffic sensors, represented as nodes in a graph [MATH G] . The traffic data is structured as a multivariate time series tensor [MATH \mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{in}}\times N\times K}] , where [MATH T_{\text{in}}] is the number of historical time steps, [MATH N] is the number of sensors, and [MATH K=1] is the traffic speed at each sensor. The aim is to develop a mapping function [MATH f] that predicts the next [MATH T_{\text{out}}] time steps of traffic conditions:
[MATH f(\mathbf{D}_{t-T_{\text{in}}+1:t},G)=\mathbf{D}_{t+1:t+T_{\text{out}}},]
(22)
where [MATH \mathbf{D}_{t+1:t+T_{\text{out}}}] represents the predicted traffic conditions for [MATH T_{\text{out}}] steps ahead. The function [MATH f^{(i)}] in Eq 6 is modeled using a linear transformation followed by a one-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Cho et al. (2014) to capture sequential patterns of traffic time series. To model spatial dependencies across the network, the same GCN as in the Earthquake predition task is used. The objective of the model is to minimize the L1 loss:
[MATH \mathcal{L}_{traffic}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\overline{\mathbf{Y}}-\mathbf{Y}\|_{1}]
(23)
where [MATH \mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{out}}\times N\times 1}] is the ground truth of traffic signals.
