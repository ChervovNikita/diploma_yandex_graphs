2.1 Preliminaries: Problem and Notation
The goal of multi-task learning is to find parameters [MATH:\theta] of a model [MATH:f_{\theta}] that achieve high average performance across all the training tasks drawn from a distribution of tasks [MATH:p(\mathcal{T})] .
More formally, we aim to solve the problem: [MATH:\min\limits_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{T}_{i}\sim p(\mathcal{T})}\left[\mathcal{L}_{i}(\theta)\right]] , where [MATH:\mathcal{L}_{i}] is a loss function for the [MATH:i] -th task [MATH:\mathcal{T}_{i}] that we want to minimize. For a set of tasks, [MATH:\{\mathcal{T}_{i}\}] , we denote the multi-task loss as [MATH:\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\sum_{i}\mathcal{L}_{i}(\theta)] , and the gradients of each task as [MATH:\mathbf{g}_{i}=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{i}(\theta)] for a particular [MATH:\theta] . (We drop the reliance on [MATH:\theta] in the notation for brevity.)
To obtain a model that solves a specific task from the task distribution [MATH:p(\mathcal{T})] , we define a task-conditioned model [MATH:f_{\theta}(y|x,z_{i})] , with input [MATH:x] , output [MATH:y] , and encoding [MATH:z_{i}] for task [MATH:\mathcal{T}_{i}] , which could be provided as a one-hot vector or in any other form.
