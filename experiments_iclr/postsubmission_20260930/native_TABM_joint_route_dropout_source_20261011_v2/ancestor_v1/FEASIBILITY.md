## What the variance argument establishes

Three perfect matchings are compared coordinatewise using detached pair covariance cost. Same-sign products use antithetic uniforms; opposite signs share an indicator. At q=.8 their mask covariances are −.04/+.16, so every activation-product covariance contribution is nonpositive. The ideal four-route linear mean at the controlled hook has no greater conditional variance than IID.

At the original stem, GELU and first LayerNorm intervene before graph aggregation. A post-GELU-only relocation would still leave LayerNorm before SAGE and would not establish the message claim. The chosen new hook is *after* that first LayerNorm. Conditional on normalized inputs, common graph support/mean aggregation and the native shared neighbor/root maps act linearly, as do explicit self concatenation and the first FFN linear map. Thus the local covariance inequality extends only along this common linear segment, before subsequent stochastic/nonlinear operations. Native SAGE's standard mean path with no optional projection/output normalization is the intended bound provider configuration and requires future runtime binding.

Later FFN dropout/GELU, residual normalization, different route heads and training dynamics are outside the argument. It is no accuracy, NLL, gradient or learned-model guarantee. Four-copy M1 has identical deterministic prefix values, making its sign pairing a conventional antithetic context rather than a private-member mechanism.
