## What function-space response would mean

On a fixed finite context U, let z_m collect class-centered logits and J_m=D_phi z_m be the complete private Jacobian at fixed theta. Define V_m=range(J_m). For a linear gauge T, J'_m=J_m T^-1, so V'_m=V_m. A supervised desired correction projected onto V_m therefore cannot acquire member heterogeneity merely by changing this gauge.

For example, with a declared positive output metric G on centered logits, a=gradient_z meanCE_S extended by zeros off S, and Pi_m the G-orthogonal projector onto V_m, the quadratic local function-space step is

    w_m = Pi_m G^-1 a;
    delta_z_m = -tau w_m.

Its predicted competitor-margin response is gradient_z b_i^T delta_z_m. A fixed output-radius alternative normalizes w_m by its G norm when that norm is nonzero. These are familiar projected-gradient/trust-region devices; writing an inverse Fisher or natural-gradient expression would not establish novelty. A probability/KL metric and a centered-logit metric encode different budgets. U, metric, radius, supervision and the tangent approximation remain declared choices, so even this construction is not a universal intrinsic-capacity certificate.

The local function-space optimum is invariant because it is defined on the attainable output subspace. An arbitrary minimum-coordinate-norm lift, Euclidean parameter damping or an incompletely solved parameter-space system can reintroduce coordinate dependence, especially when J has the proved gauge null directions. A finite nonlinear step requires a covariant lift/solver and actual before/after confirmation; first-order output invariance alone proves neither. Constructing J or solving its metric system on the native full graph can be expensive. No such solver or arm is proposed for the current pilot.

## Why a corrective signal could help a common error

If all members currently rank the same wrong class above the true class, every nonnegative probability mixture retains that pairwise ordering. Correction must change member predictions. Yet agreeing predictions do not force equal private tangent subspaces or equal transfer of a supervised step: the private stem precedes graph propagation. When S supervision has a component in a member's reachable corrective direction, its update can reduce a true-versus-wrong margin. A response allocation could emphasize those productive corrections while the all-member own-CE anchor maintains general competence.

This mechanism has explicit failure conditions. If the relevant information is already lost, a private update cannot recover it. If members have equal item-wise responses, centering eliminates the allocation signal and the balanced solver stays uniform; ordinary own-CE learning can still correct the error. If the S correction harms R, or if balance/graph smoothing allocates mass away from useful corrections, the response mechanism can fail despite invariant costs. Lower mean S CE also does not guarantee improvement of every competitor margin. Neither gauge invariance, a large response nor diversity of tangents proves correction.

The plausible extension is therefore **reachable corrective response with controlled output movement and demonstrated transfer**, not the optimizer formula. It remains unresolved whether that extension adds anything beyond saved gradient-utility and ordinary supervised-learning antecedents.
