# Prediction guided factors and the competence coverage tradeoff

The [independent Wiki24 interpretation](/Users/alex/Documents/ChatGPT/anogena allocation/postsubmission_research_20260930/wikics24_scientific_interpretation_independent_20261007_v1/REPORT.md) supports a narrow diagnosis: unit-plus-contrast gains mostly member competence; Rademacher initialization gains coverage while weakening members, and the primary contrast does not reliably preserve competence. This motivates better controlled asymmetry. It does not establish a graph-operator defect or make prediction-guided initialization a solution.

## What near unit initialization tests

A first factor close to one perturbs the original shared computation less than a sign-flipping factor. It may reduce damage while retaining some stochastic route asymmetry. Smaller perturbations can also disappear under shared training and leave redundant members. Their final competence, coverage and pool balance cannot be inferred from initial radius alone.

**Near-unit scaling is an initialization tuning ablation**, within BatchEnsemble/TabM ancestry. A positive result would identify a useful operating point for this architecture and training recipe. It would not introduce a new diversity mechanism. Root reports that Citeseer initialization21 already compared warm identity, first-normal, all-sign and graph/feature covariance at fixed factor RMS1, with failure. Repeating those conditions unchanged is unwarranted. Changing the displacement scale tests a different hyperparameter; it does not convert that earlier family into a new principle or establish transfer to WikiCS.

## What the saved prediction guided fallback adds

The [saved fallback](/Users/alex/Documents/ChatGPT/anogena allocation/postsubmission_research_20260930/predictive_tangent_internal_BE_initialization_assessment_20261007_v1/NOTE.md) asks whether non-null prediction-guided directions in **existing internal factors**, after the same TRAIN-only warm acquisition, improve final predictions beyond random directions with matched predictive disturbance and comparable finite loss damage. That is a more meaningful orientation comparison than equal factor RMS: multiplicative gauges, normalization and different sensitivities can make equal RMS produce very different function changes.

It could address the tradeoff by avoiding ineffective directions and controlling initial competence damage. It cannot ensure useful coverage. Model-Fisher visibility includes confidence changes with unchanged decisions; observed true-label gradients can emphasize noisy/hard examples or vanish on confident TRAIN labels. Leading sensitivity directions can damage competence and be damped rapidly by continuation. Flat directions can preserve TRAIN loss while producing arbitrary extrapolation. Centered offsets preserve only the first-order common function; nonlinear gates can still damage individual members and move the mean at higher order. The eligible factor span may contain no disturbance that both preserves competence and repairs common competitors.

A task-relevant margin change must alter truth-versus-competitor ordering or the declared task's comparison, rather than merely temperature or hidden-vector distance. For WikiCS, reducing common wrong-competitor support is more informative than at-least-one-correct coverage alone: the existing interpretation shows that pooling can still lose available correct evidence. TRAIN-only geometry and finite admission cannot guarantee the selected development or unseen population's ranking behavior.

## Difference from the closest principles

Jacobian/tangent ensembles, Local Ensembles, curvature-aware shallow ensembles and adapter-Laplace work already establish prediction geometry, constrained/flat directions and anisotropic sampling. FoRDE already targets task-conditioned functional diversity through true-label input-gradient repulsion. The saved fallback changes the operational setting to a one-time displacement of existing internal BE coordinates followed by a live common core. FoRDE instead changes training continuously and differentiates input-gradient interactions. This distinction is real; it does not make predictive geometry or competence-preserving diversity new.

Graph/feature covariance at RMS1 tests a particular orientation and scale. It does not settle a matched **actual predictor disturbance** comparison. Conversely, a future tangent initializer gain over those old conditions could be explained entirely by milder perturbation or warm acquisition. Warm identity-copy continuation and equally disturbed random factor directions remain necessary for the saved orientation claim; no identical repeat of initialization21, new tangent framework, or extra objective is recommended here.

## Disposition

Retain the existing fallback as an **unestablished orientation hypothesis**, not a demonstrated remedy. A material result would require final served gains and net common-competitor repairs while protecting mean and worst-member competence, beyond the same warm continuation and matched random disturbance. Useful neighbor/evidence-margin differences would be needed for a graph mechanism claim. Greater initial spread, more coverage, a TRAIN certificate, or a narrow cohort gain would be insufficient.

Near-unit first-factor scaling remains the cheaper tuning explanation to distinguish from any expensive predictive-geometry claim. Neither the Wiki24 tradeoff nor the saved fallback authorizes a radius sweep, framework expansion, new fit, or change to the planned MolHIV I/BEfirstsign study. Root retains admission and priority decisions.
