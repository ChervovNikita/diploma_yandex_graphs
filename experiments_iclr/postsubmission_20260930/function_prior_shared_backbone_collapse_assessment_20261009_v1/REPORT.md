## What can and cannot be guaranteed

**Frozen additive prior.** Write `z_m(x)=f_(theta,eta_m)(x)+beta p_m(x)`, with p_m genuinely fixed over training. Own task loss remains on the total prediction. If the trainable residual can represent `g(x)−beta p_m(x)`, all total predictions can collapse to g. Fixed priors therefore supply persistent forcing/inductive bias, not an anti-collapse theorem. If p is recomputed from a live shared representation, stopping its gradient does not freeze its numerical function; that is a moving target, not the inspected randomized prior.

For an analytic shared linear regression basis B and residual ridge lambda, minimizing `||B w_m+beta p_m−y||²+lambda||w_m||²` gives

`z_m*=S_lambda y+beta(I−S_lambda)p_m`,

`S_lambda=B(B^T B+lambda I)^−1 B^T`.

Prior variation survives only where this smoother does not cancel it. With unregularized full row-rank B, `S=I`: every prediction equals y. If priors are exactly member-centered, their **linear prediction mean is S_lambda y**, exactly the prior-free ridge mean even when individual predictions remain diverse. Diversity then gives no mean-prediction gain. If `p_m=B a_m`, the additive construction is just a shifted head parameterization and residual decay is an anchor at `beta a_m`. A graph-conditioned prior in the same fixed feature span does not evade that existing algebra. Finite nonlinear softmax pooling and joint representation learning differ, but need evidence rather than a transferred guarantee.

**Explicit functional anchor.** A penalty `lambda/(2M|Q|) sum_(m,q) ||h_m(q)−a_m(q)||²` can sustain distinct targets a_m on a TRAIN-only probe population Q. For softmax, h should remove class-common logit shifts; for independent Bernoulli labels it must not remove a common label-logit shift. At an identical-member state with common private Jacobian J and member-centered targets, the private anchor force is `−lambda J^T a_m`; the common-core contrast force sums to zero. Targets in the private Jacobian nullspace do not break symmetry. Shared capacity, finite optimization and projection can still block separation. Reachable distinct output targets resist exact probe-output collapse, not representation collapse elsewhere.

A task-fit counterexample is already decisive: minimizing `(u_m−y)²+lambda(u_m−a_m)²` gives `u_m=(y+lambda a_m)/(1+lambda)`. Distinct a_m force distinct predictions, but generally worsen member fit; even centered anchors shrink the mean y. Random classification anchors can similarly force wrong margins. Anchoring random initial predictions is not retaining a competent teacher. Anchoring member deviations around their mean frees the common component, but merely introduces fixed disagreement targets; it does not establish informative specialization. A finite TRAIN probe set also permits differences only on the probes, memorization, or unseen-input collapse.

This is materially different from identity-centered factor L2 **as a metric and forcing target**, not as a novel prior principle. Linearization pulls function penalties back to familiar `J^T W J` quadratic forms. It avoids some factor-gauge/hidden-embedding ambiguities but still rewards confidence or nuisance directions unless task-visible responses are measured. FoRDE, parameter anchors, NTK function priors and graph-GP covariances already cover its ancestry.
