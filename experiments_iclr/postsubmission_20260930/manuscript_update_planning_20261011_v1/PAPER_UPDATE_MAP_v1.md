## Proposed plain explanations

> Original GNNM distinguishes members at the input and output maps around shared graph layers. The internal-factor version also places member factors in dense maps within those layers. Members can therefore transform their hidden states differently during propagation. Both versions retain shared weight matrices and still require a graph pass for each member.

> Coverage counts nodes for which at least one member predicts the correct class. Averaging can lose some of those correct alternatives. A bank with weaker members on average can still cover more useful alternatives and produce a better pooled prediction. We report coverage and pooling losses together.

> The Roman SAGE gain comes from better individual members. The F development result instead combines weaker average members with broader coverage and higher pooled accuracy. These describe selected training procedures; they do not identify error covariance, collapse avoidance or a universal cause of the gain.

Keep these explanations declarative and brief. Established factorization remains prior work; any later contribution is the supported empirical comparison and its scope, not an invented successful primitive.
