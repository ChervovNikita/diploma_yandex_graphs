# From prediction errors to a corrective training test

We want the shared ensemble to make more accurate predictions. The completed Amazon comparison shows two weaknesses: its members are slightly weaker than independent members, and averaging adds much less accuracy. Improving disagreement alone would not address both.

## What the predictions show

Across three selected validation panels, averaging adds 0.16 accuracy points to the shared members and 0.59 points to independent members. All four members are wrong on 93.60% of shared pooled errors, versus 74.86% of independent pooled errors. Shared wrong predictions are also more confident. A selector that chooses one member's predicted class cannot fix the cases where every member is wrong.

The class, degree and graph-neighborhood analysis is complete. It finds stronger shared unanimous agreement around a narrow population of common mistakes, but no larger general recurrence of the pooled wrong class. That observation does not establish that the shared backbone loses information or spreads errors. The saved nonlinear aggregation comparison also fails against equally processed independent and capable single references.

## The concrete intervention being prepared

Every member continues to learn every training label. Additional supervision asks it to distinguish the true class from each competing class. We allocate that additional pressure using a measured response: which member reduces that distinction's loss after one small private learning step. Responsibilities are balanced, and a fixed graph affinity can make nearby training nodes use consistent specialist identities. All class pairs and both label directions are included.

The shared backbone then receives training credit through that finite private response. Inference averages member probabilities. This targets useful learning capacity and the quality of the served prediction. It does not assume that arbitrary embedding separation improves accuracy.

## How it can fail, and how we will tell

| Explanation | Discriminating comparison |
|---|---|
|Extra supervision is sufficient|Identical uniform extra competitor supervision|
|Assigning to the currently strongest member is sufficient|Assignment by current competitor loss|
|Graph alignment adds no useful signal|No-affinity and one fixed graph-permutation controls|
|Learning through responsibility has no useful effect|Same forward assignment with its credit path stopped|
|The method weakens members to create disagreement|Complete individual-member accuracy and loss alongside pooled quality|

The response comparison uses a fresh training-only state and a fixed held training population excluded from all acquisition and episode fitting. Before acquisition, we have adopted a separate warm/support/outer training-label split: support and outer labels are absent from warmup and first enter learning during the correction episodes. They are training supervision thereafter. The sealed V1 proposal remains preserved; a separate V2 caller and exact compute budget are being prepared. [Decision](CORRECTIVE_LEARNING_PREFIT_DECISION_20261005.md).

Outcomes from all fixed arms are retained before comparison. A useful pilot must improve the full probability pool while preserving member competence. Subsequent representative comparisons still need ordinary independent ensembles and a capable same-capacity model trained directly with ordinary own/pool objectives.
