# Independent assessment of the complete closed Wiki24 degree profile

Assessment date: 8 October 2026. This is an assessment of the saved descriptive scientific result and its relationship to a proposed learning rule. It makes no acceptance, novelty, significance or causal verdict. The analysis used the immutable execution packet `closed_wiki24_graph_error_profile_execution_root_20261008_v1/{PROFILE.json,COHORT_FREEZE.json,INPUTS.json,TERMINAL.json,FETCH_CUSTODY.json}` and the source packet `closed_wiki24_graph_error_profile_source_20261008_v1/{profile.py,README.md,INPUT_CONTRACT.json}`. Only the proposed method and comparison specification in `contrastive_BE_graph_training_rule_synthesis_20261008_v1.md` informed the method discussion. No author disposition or conversation history informed the conclusions.

The saved result is internally consistent and answers a useful descriptive question: where the two selected banks differ in correctness, correct-member coverage and common ranking errors across a fixed degree/class population. Ordinary independent ensembles have more correct-member coverage in every marginal degree cohort and every seed. Their much smaller served gains show that available correct rankings and successful probability pooling are different outcomes. High incoming degree is associated with fewer old-bank all-member errors overall, but the within-class pattern is heterogeneous, including persistent reversals for classes 7 and 8. These observations make class-conditioned graph regimes a concrete descriptive question. They do not evaluate the proposed specialization rule or establish why the errors occurred.

The consumer verifies these argmax identities and uses the authoritative original pooled predictions. It neither averages logits nor uses voting, a best-member oracle or a recomputed CPU softmax. Static custody checks bind the original collector's `mean_probability` convention. The old bank uses one coherent joint checkpoint per seed, at epochs 129/127/134; the independent bank contains separately selected checkpoints at epochs 133/98/132/145, 127/134/98/151, and 100/150/142/133. Old members all retain global mode, while the independent bank retains one local-mode member in each seed. Thus the comparison is between these actual trained and selected banks; it does not isolate only sharing, only contrastive regularization, only checkpoint selection or only serving mode.

The following identity explains why the coverage advantage produces much less served advantage:

`served correct = any-member correct − pool harm + pool rescue`.

Pool harm here means a bank's own pool is wrong despite at least one correct member. It is distinct from a paired served harm caused by switching banks.

This does not mean pooling is inherently unable to rescue all-wrong members in other states: a probability mean can do so when members' wrong rankings differ. It describes these saved states.

Acquisition counts must not be relabeled served repairs. The profile supplies aggregate acquisition and served transitions, but does not give the exact cross-tabulation of destination served correctness within acquired old all-member errors. From the recorded counts alone, between 111 and 123, 123 and 131, and 110 and 119 old all-member-error acquisitions are served correctly by the ordinary bank: at most 12/8/9 served repairs could originate from old pool harms. Consequently at least 116/104/127 acquired-correct-member nodes remain unserved. An exact acquisition-to-serving conversion fraction cannot be obtained by simply dividing all served repairs by all acquisitions.

**Strict common rivals.** Every old all-member-error node also has a strict common rival: a wrong class strictly exceeds truth in every original member logit vector. The ordinary common-rival counts are 734/738/745, slightly below its 750/752/752 all-member errors. This distinction is implemented correctly. A common rival is a stronger condition than merely all members being wrong; unanimous argmax and strict common ranking are distinct properties. In exact arithmetic, softmax preserves each member's ordering, so a single shared strict rival also beats truth in the probability mean. The actual saved counts contain no common-rival node with a correct pool.

For the ordinary destination on the old common-rival population, one member strictly beats every old common rival on 247/241/251 nodes, while every old common rival is beaten by some member on 255/250/256. The latter event occurs without any correct destination argmax on 16/15/10 nodes. Beating the old rivals can still leave a new rival above truth, and different destination members can defeat different old rivals. The separate counts correctly prevent either reversal event from being treated as correct-member acquisition. The banks retain at least one identical strict common rival on 704/699/713 nodes; both have some strict common rival on 707/702/716 nodes. Large surviving common errors describe overlap, but do not identify a graph mechanism.

**What this contributes to subsequent specialization.** The exact proposed rule ranks TRAIN nodes by incoming degree separately within each observed class, breaks ties by canonical TRAIN identity, allocates quarter-class masses to four routes with fractional boundary nodes, and sets `w(i,m)=0.5+2q(i,m)`. Since each row's q mass is one and each member receives one quarter of each class's mass, the mean route weight per node is one and every member's weighted class total equals the unweighted class total. Those algebraic statements are correct. The shared block receives unweighted mean-member supervised CE derivatives, while private factors receive weighted same-state derivatives before one Adam transition; serving stays a uniform mean of all full-route probabilities. In general this block rule is not the gradient of one scalar loss. None of those derivatives, trajectories or prospective outcomes is tested by the saved profile.

Its regimes are not the regimes profiled here. This result uses common TRAIN-derived global degree thresholds with degree ties intact, applied to development nodes. The proposal uses within-class TRAIN ranks, potentially assigns equal-degree nodes different emphasis by identity, and fractionally splits class-mass boundaries. The available compact result does not supply the within-class TRAIN degree distribution needed to translate its global bins into those allocations. It cannot validate the chosen four routes, equal quartiles, half anchor, fractional allocation, recipient factors or shared/private credit split. Class balance prevents the proposed weights from changing class totals, but does not by itself isolate structural specialization or protect global serving competence.

The observed conditional error variation gives a concrete reason to ask whether fixed class-balanced structural emphasis can change learned correctness overlap. It gives no evidence that this particular assignment will add correct rankings, retain member quality, improve probability pooling, or remedy the old contrastive bank's error overlap. Ordinary banks obtain their larger coverage across all degree bins without this assignment. The compared old and ordinary banks also differ in training, checkpoint selection and local/global composition. Moving from their descriptive contrast to weighted private supervised risk introduces a new intervention, whose effects cannot be read off these counts.

A subsequent prospective comparison would need to evaluate actual served accuracy and probability quality on complete populations while retaining member competence, not pass on greater spread or oracle coverage alone. Uniform private CE and a frozen within-class shuffled assignment provide direct comparators for the proposed weighting and degree correspondence; the ordinary independent bank remains a necessary capable reference. To attribute a benefit to specialization under sharing, the corresponding untied assignment and anchored allocation alternatives matter as well. These are requirements for interpreting a future result, not conclusions that such a result will be favorable or permission to run it. The current profile contains no NLL/Brier cohort summaries or intervention results, and supplies no new probability-quality conclusion.

All three blocks concern one graph, one official split and the same 5,274 nodes; routes and outcomes are correlated. Both banks' checkpoints were selected using this merged validation/stopping development role, and they were then diagnosed on it. Frozen TRAIN-only thresholds avoid tuning bins to the saved errors, but do not remove checkpoint-selection optimism or turn an exploratory diagnosis into unused confirmation evidence. Three consistent descriptive signs are useful evidence about these complete saved states, not three independent graphs or 15,822 independent observations. Subsequent unchanged-rule confirmation requires evidence whose use history permits it.

**Material mistakes and practical interpretation.** I found no material arithmetic, cohort-denominator, acquisition-definition or serving-definition mistake in the inspected compact result or its implementation. The source correctly keeps ordinary coverage acquisition, paired served repairs/harms, pool harms/rescues and common-rival reversals distinct. It retains the original selected states and probabilities. Its successful recorded reconciliation to earlier authenticated integer summaries is part of the saved execution evidence; this assessment independently reproduced identities within the profile, rather than reopening the earlier numeric archives.

The material interpretation errors to avoid are treating a 197–217-node net coverage advantage as a comparable served improvement; treating common-rival reversal as correct argmax acquisition; identifying high global degree as uniformly easier after looking only at marginal bins; calling incoming-zero nodes wholly devoid of graph or model information; treating same-development selected diagnostics as confirmation; and treating the global-bin description as a test of the exact within-class allocation rule. The complete result supports a bounded descriptive statement about correlated error structure and pooling in these selected populations. It leaves the benefits and mechanism of subsequent graph-regime specialization empirically open.
