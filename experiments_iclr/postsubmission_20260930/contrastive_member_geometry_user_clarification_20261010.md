# Contrast inside shared-backbone ensemble members

10 October 2026. This note answers the user's renewed suggestion and records the distinction so the completed experiments are not repeated. It adds no method, new reading, execution admission or positive result.

The shared backbone can remain trainable and shared while a contrastive objective acts on member-specific intermediate representations. A shared weight does not require identical activations: private BatchEnsemble factors and the different upstream member states can change the input to that weight. The choice of positive and negative pairs determines whether this encourages class discrimination, member identity, or useful task complementarity.

Within a member, supervised contrast brings compatible examples or views together and separates different labelled classes. Pushing all node representations apart would also repel same-class and same-node positives. Graph adjacency alone does not establish a valid positive pair on heterophilous graphs. Across members, treating the same node's other member representations as negatives encourages member differences but does not ensure that any member acquires a correct prediction.

The complete WikiCS split0 development comparison already includes within-member class alignment, canonical supervised contrast, class-centered between-member contrast, and their combination. Combined minus plain accuracy averaged -0.0695234 percentage points; canonical SupCon minus plain averaged -0.19593. These are three optimizer seeds on one reused selected-development population, with wide descriptive intervals. They neither support the fitted recipe nor rule out every contrastive method. The report did not establish that hidden embeddings collapsed or that their separation succeeded. Source: `Wiki12_SupCon15_union_collection_execution_root_20261008_v1/compact/REPORT.md`.

Different embedding coordinates can preserve the same predictor. For example, rotating a hidden representation and compensating in its classifier changes hidden distances without changing logits. This illustrates why angular separation alone is not the task outcome. Private-only auxiliary gradients are implementable and already prepared/tested in this research, but do not guarantee preservation of individual member competence.

The complete attention-credit study also found highly redundant predictions: relation-credit members agree on roughly 98.4–98.6% of development nodes. Independent members have broader correct-member coverage and substantially greater pooling lift, under the disclosed historical provider/selector differences. This is evidence about predictions, not a causal diagnosis of hidden collapse. Source: `relation18_completed_scientific_decision_root_20261010_v1/INTERPRETATION.md`.

For any future contrastive extension, specify the actual representation and pair semantics, preserve ordinary label supervision, and compare to an otherwise identical no-contrast model. Measure served accuracy/NLL, individual competence, errors repaired and introduced, and acquired useful alternatives. Use a capable genuine independent ensemble reference and unused confirmation before a superiority claim. A graph-specific claim additionally needs evidence that members use meaningfully different graph information. Embedding spread or a lower auxiliary loss alone cannot meet these requirements.

The proposed wrong-class-output contrast is an unimplemented attributed ADP/CDLG composition with a member class-bias shortcut and exact binary degeneration, not an established novelty. Moving a previously tested loss to another internal site is not automatically a new mechanism. The standard independent local-scorer initializer is a reviewed diagnostic control, not new initialization theory. Pending Q/K selected-state collection is the next scientific readout; no new contrastive grid is admitted by this note.
