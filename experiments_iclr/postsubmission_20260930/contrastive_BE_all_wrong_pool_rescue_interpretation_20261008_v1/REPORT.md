# Correct pooling without a correct individual member

8 October 2026. Interpretation of saved logic and prior conclusions only. No scientific arrays, partial study outcomes, model execution, new fit or gate change.

## Two legitimate repairs, with different meanings

A model can remove a common-rival failure by acquiring a correct member, or by producing sufficiently complementary class evidence that its fixed pool becomes correct while every member remains wrong. Both can improve served accuracy. Neither is established by embedding separation, increased disagreement or a reversal against only an old rival.

For truth class y and member probabilities p_m, define B_m as the rival classes c with p_m(c)>p_m(y). A class in the intersection of all B_m strictly defeats truth in every member and therefore also in any normalized nonnegative probability pool. Removing that intersection is necessary for a strictly correct probability mean, but is not sufficient. The equal probability mean is strictly correct exactly when, for every rival c,

    sum_m [p_m(y) - p_m(c)] > 0.

Different members may supply positive margins against different rivals; no one member has to beat every rival. The corresponding condition for mean raw logits uses z_m(y)-z_m(c), not probability differences. Pooling rules must remain separate. These statements concern strict inequalities; native argmax tie handling remains the declared scoring contract.

The existing fabricated counterexample already demonstrates the second route. Members 0/2 give (truth, rival1, rival2)=(.32,.40,.08); members 1/3 give (.32,.08,.40). Each assigns the remaining .20 equally to seven other classes. Every member is wrong, yet the probability mean gives truth .32 and both rivals .24. This is an already saved logic check, not a new experiment or performance observation. The saved counterexample with (.25,.60,.02) and (.25,.02,.60) also shows why different rival reversals alone are insufficient: both rival means .31 still defeat truth .25.

## Preserve the direct12 decision

The published direct12 clarification keeps its acquired-correct-member screen. Do not replace that screen after seeing a pooled-only improvement. Report separately:

1. Native all-member-wrong nodes acquiring at least one actually correct fitted member.
2. Native pooled-wrong nodes repaired by the fitted pool while every fitted member remains wrong.
3. Old-rival ordering reversals without an actual correct member or a pooled repair.

A result of type 2 can be scientifically useful while failing the declared type-1 screen. It should remain a recorded result and can motivate a separately reviewed, prospective cooperation hypothesis. It cannot retrospectively pass direct12's frozen screen. Retain pooled harms, lost member coverage, new common rivals and whole-population member/pool quality; favorable error-cohort counts alone do not establish benefit. Three optimizer seeds on one selected development split do not provide independent confirmation.

For a binary task with strict individual errors, all members necessarily prefer the same sole rival. The multiclass all-wrong-but-correct-pool route is then unavailable. This is an argmax statement, not an ROC-AUC statement; do not transfer it to Mol18's ranking endpoint.

## Prior filter before proposing cooperation

Saved conclusions already cover the main ingredients:

- **ADP**, Pang et al., arXiv:1901.08846v3, §§3.1–3.3: normalize non-target probability vectors and promote their Gram log determinant, alongside ensemble entropy/member CE. It directly precedes deliberate differentiation of wrong-class evidence. Its original setting is adversarial robustness with probability pooling. Bare logdet is dimensionally invalid when M>C−1; C10/M4 avoids that particular obstruction but does not establish graph utility or novelty.
- **GNCL**, Buschjäger et al., arXiv:2011.02952v2, §4.1 Eq.5: interpolate member risk and actual collective risk. A member/pool loss mixture is already established. Its inspected author code applies the scalar mixture globally; the project's internal-factor permission allocation is a separately specified composition.
- **sMCL/CMCL**, saved primary scopes in the competitor-responsibility assessment: minimum-loss ownership and confidence-controlled specialization are established. Their inspected hard ownership is different from the project's positive balanced responsibilities, but that difference does not certify a new principle. Oracle member selection is different from the fixed deployed mean.

Mean individual CE and probability-mean CE have identical scalar values under any exact redistribution of wrong-class mass that leaves every p_m(y) fixed. This does not mean their parameter gradients are identical across such states or that ordinary CE training cannot redistribute rival mass. It does mean neither scalar value alone measures whether the largest pooled rival was suppressed. Full-class pooled Brier/margin objectives and ADP supply existing ways to address rival distributions; generic competitor cooperation must be attributed rather than presented as a new discovery.

The project already saved a learnability-weighted graph-responsibility operator using competitor margins, finite private responses, balanced graph assignments and shared-core credit. It remains an existing, separately guarded proposal; this note does not reissue or admit it. First close the currently declared target/alignment/internal-credit/head studies. A supported pooled-only finding can justify one deliberately specified comparison against relevant established cooperative/diverse-ensemble controls, with member competence and actual serving quality retained.
