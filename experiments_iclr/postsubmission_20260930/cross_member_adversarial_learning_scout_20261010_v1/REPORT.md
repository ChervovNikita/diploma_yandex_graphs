## Protected graph counterexamples were considered and rejected

An aggregate recipient-risk guard can hide new errors at individual nodes because
one feature change influences many neighboring predictions. Projecting an attack
away from recipient loss gradients only protects a first-order quantity. It does
not certify finite margins or local correctness. At identical routes, no
producer-exclusive gradient direction may exist. Normalizing a tiny residual
would manufacture apparent attack magnitude without evidence of useful alternatives.

Actual finite protection would require binding each affected TRAIN endpoint's
decision/margin, testing all recipients at each trial, and preserving every failure.
That is many full-graph derivative/verification operations. No feasible complete
rule, justified label-preserving perturbation domain or useful competence mechanism
was established here. The additional projection/guard machinery is not promoted.
