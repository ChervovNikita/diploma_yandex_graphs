## Exact scalar-gauge obstruction

Use only the proved scalar gauge of the current private factors:

    R' = aR, S' = S/a, B' = B, a != 0.

Do not assume an arbitrary matrix, per-coordinate diagonal or orthogonal gauge is valid for the actual fast-vector architecture. Let phi'=T phi be a fixed valid invertible linear transformation and write the invariant objective in the new coordinates as L'(phi')=L(T^-1 phi'). A Euclidean SGD step gives

    grad_phi' L' = T^-T grad_phi L,
    phi_next = phi - eta (T^-1 T^-T) grad_phi L.

This is an exact coordinate identity for each step under a fixedT and the same invariant objective. For the scalar factor gauge the effective group rates are

    eta_R = eta/a^2, eta_S = eta*a^2, eta_B = eta.

The shared-backbone gradient at corresponding states remains unchanged. A fixed gauge therefore implements a private preconditioner. Once ensemble functions diverge, their averaged shared gradient need not equal one single model's gradient; the argument does not claim finite whole-ensemble equivalence to one single predictor.

At a common initial function and corresponding state, write the complete private Jacobian as [J_R,J_S,J_B]. Its kernel is

    K_a = a^-2 K_R + a^2 K_S + K_B.

An invertible coordinate transformation leaves range(J) unchanged. It changes the metric on attainable corrections, not the attainable first-order subspace. With the same training examples, masks and normalized member objective, the first-order movement of the probability mean equals the movement generated by the mean metric/kernel, plus the common shared contribution if trained. Any mean-loss1/M convention is a common factor that the control's rates must include. The softmax/sigmoid differential is common at identical initial predictions, so this conclusion applies to the actual first-order probability pool, not merely raw logits.

A pair a=exp(t),exp(-t) gives mean coefficients cosh(2t) for bothR andS, whileB keeps coefficient1. Thus even a symmetric gauge bank can accelerate the mean private step. A uniformly stepped native single would confound this scale with an ensemble effect. A single using the mean group rates reproduces the pooled first-order correction; individually preconditioned single/independent trajectories reproduce the member operation. The gauges+1/-1 give the identity metric and provide no ordinary-SGD functional trajectory diversity under matched masks/state.

Finite nonlinear trajectories can later differ and their probability average can help or harm. That possibility is a hypothesis about ensemble utility; it is not a new reachable-capacity theorem. The algebra does not extend automatically to Adam, coordinate regularization, dynamically changing gauges, or unmatched optimizer/momentum state. It also supplies no floating-point equality guarantee. GAUGE_DERIVATION.json records these boundaries.
