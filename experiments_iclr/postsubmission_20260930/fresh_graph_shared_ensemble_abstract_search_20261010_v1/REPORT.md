# Targeted current-literature refresh

10 October 2026. Two official arXiv API searches returned11 graph/shared-ensemble records and25 cross-domain efficient-ensemble records. These are discovery abstracts, not full-paper reads. No new primary-read credit, novelty certificate, experiment or new direction is earned.

The graph search rediscovered the May2026 graph uncertainty-collapse study and September2026 GVBLL. Their already saved primary conclusions were reused. The collapse paper restricts its claims to the examined message-passing models and leaves functional convexity as a hypothesis. GVBLL uses a deterministic graph encoder and variational/online Bayesian last layer; it receives later streaming labels, so its online accuracy claims cannot be transplanted into a fixed-label node-classification comparison.

The cross-domain search rediscovered normalization-based implicit ensembles, parameter-isolated experts, TabLoRA, LoMETab, LoRA-Ensemble and TabM. These already constrain any claim that shared bodies plus private factors, low-rank correction or diversity shaping are new primitives. New late-September class-incremental LoRA prototype/teacher titles remain abstract-only leads. Their different tasks, pretrained backbones, later task labels or teacher ensembles do not supply a ready explanation of our fixed graph errors. No pilot is opened from those abstracts.

The active source question remains whether a shared graph body can support useful private neighborhood-distribution correctors beyond a capable richer single and genuinely independent counterparts. PNA/FSW are acknowledged closest ingredient priors. A stronger literature context is not performance evidence.
