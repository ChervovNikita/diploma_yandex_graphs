This concerns which interventions identify a predictor, not whether those differences are useful. ## Concrete discriminating statement

`THEORY_AND_DISCRIMINATING_CHECK.md` proves three local statements without executing model code:

1. For scalar linear filters, feature-only input/readout gates give proportional coefficient vectors; order gates can give nonproportional vectors with O(M(K+1)) private scalars. This restriction does not generalize to nonlinear or multichannel BE.
2. A two-node degree-two predictor `z_m=sX+b_m(P²−P)X` is identical across members at the native averaging graph and at complete edge deletion, including its native full-feature Jacobian. On the normalized lazy-walk family `P_t=(1−t)P+tI`, its topology derivative is `−b_m X`. The two-node fixed-loop edge weight is `(1−t)/(1+t)`, not `1−t`.
3. K+1 distinct lazy-walk points identify a degree-K linear response curve by ordinary polynomial interpolation. Recovery of its scalar filter needs a visible nonconstant eigenmode. The exact binomial transform of cached `P^j H_0` computes the entire degree-K token bank for these interventions without new graph propagation. A nonlinear row-local head may consume those exact tokens, but its output need not be degree K and has no K+1 identification guarantee.

The analytic discriminator is the witness at `{0, 1/2, 1}` versus a readout-nullspace hidden-code change. The former changes class probabilities at the interior point; the latter changes no probability anywhere in the family. Native FoRDE feature gradients and the complete-deletion response are blind to the witness. The family measures a declared weighted-structure sensitivity; it does not cover arbitrary edge removals, changes of eigenvectors, causal effects, or label-preserving changes.
