# From literature to a shared-ensemble learning rule

## Objective and current evidence

The research objective is better prediction than competent single models and ordinary independent ensembles, with a defensible methodological difference. Parameter storage or increased disagreement does not satisfy that objective. No new method currently satisfies it.

The complete Citeseer-HeaRT development comparison has three paired blocks and all 36 physical fits. Unchanged shared F4 has mean VALID MRR 28.4115%, versus 27.9204% for ordinary independent four and 26.7811% for native single. Private endpoint frames score 28.1795%, below unchanged F4 by 0.2320 percentage points. The frozen frame candidate is not promoted. These validation-selected results on one graph are encouraging for existing sharing, not confirmed superiority or novelty. Every primary descriptive interval includes zero; TEST remains unopened. The prior complete Collab private-completion result also trails independent four. Preserve both outcomes.

## Literature changes the proposed mechanism

BatchEnsemble/TabM establish shared matrices, private factors and member learning. Collaborative-ensemble and learner-collusion work establishes ensemble loss and the need to retain competent members. ANIL/BMAML establishes shared features trained through private adaptation; Meta-Graph establishes graph link-prediction meta-learning. MLDG/MetaReg establishes training-only transfer with ordinary deployment. Consequently, neither a diversity loss, a shared meta-ensemble nor deployment without adaptation is a new principle.

The prioritized hypothesis is more specific: update shared features according to how four persistent private learners predict after their own learning step on other endpoints. Graph queries reuse endpoints and neighborhoods, so temporarily excluding outer-query endpoints from private inner labels could encourage useful transfer rather than repeatedly fitting the same local evidence. This is a conjecture about correlated graph supervision, not a diagnosed failure or an independence theorem.

One episode draws model-independent TRAIN outer queries, draws private TRAIN inner queries excluding those endpoints, and masks positive target edges from a common support graph. It makes virtual private updates, evaluates their mean raw-logit loss plus a fixed member-competence term on outer queries, and differentiates through the private learning step to update the shared core. It then recomputes private updates at the new shared weights and commits those parameters and optimizer states once. Inference uses the committed ordinary predictor without adaptation. The potentially different complete recipe is graph endpoint-conditioned transfer, live private-learning credit and persistent serving-state alignment. Its broader novelty remains unresolved.
