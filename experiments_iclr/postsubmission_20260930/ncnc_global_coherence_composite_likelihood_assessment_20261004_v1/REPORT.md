# J-bank coherence and composite likelihood

The frozen bank with dropout disabled **admits a coherent finite graph distribution conditional on one fixed masked context**. The current auxiliary evaluates query blocks, and its realized training calls do not maintain that deterministic edge-consistency because dropout is active. Neither fact establishes a posterior over the true graph, compatibility across different masks, or calibrated graph worlds. Calling the whole current training procedure “composite likelihood of a shared latent true-graph law” would overstate the source.

The following conclusions are about source expressions in real arithmetic, not observed equality of fitted numerical outputs. `pattern_*` files are from pilot v5; `prototype.py` and `graph_ops.py` are from completion qualification v2. Exact pins and full paths are in SOURCES.json.

**What is shared.** For a fixed masked graph G, features X, parameters and evaluation mode, the encoder gives one node bank h. Member m constructs h_m=h+xlin_m(h). A residual slot for outer query (u,v) becomes the actual counterpart pair (v,n) or (u,n), passed to `depth_zero(h_m,G,pair,m)` (`prototype.py:187–205`). There is no outer-query identifier in that scorer. Depth zero uses the counterpart endpoint product, another complete node transformation, its own common-neighbor intersection and the member decoder (`prototype.py:162–173`). Recursion stops here; it does not reconstruct a different graph for each outer block.

All factor maps and private LayerNorm parameters are fixed by m. LayerNorm operates over each feature vector, rather than across queries (`prototype.py:87–125`). GCN degree normalization depends on G, not the residual block. With dropout disabled, the second node transformation is also the same on every call. Endpoint multiplication and common-neighbor intersection are symmetric, so reversing an undirected counterpart pair leaves its real-arithmetic score unchanged. Consequently the same missing edge has the same q_m across left/right lists, different outer queries and different scoring chunks, provided G and X are fixed. Floating accumulation/shape effects and actual bitwise equality remain unverified.

Normalization is pointwise: q_m=sigmoid(2.5(s_m−6)+log(.1)); native weight is 1.05q_m (`pattern_model.py:74–79`; `prototype.py:130–132`). There is no softmax or normalization across candidate edges. The component responsibility in the objective is across members for one observed block; it does not alter q_m.

**The finite extension, with its proof.** Let E be a finite universe of undirected edges absent from G, and retain observed edges as fixed. In evaluation mode define

\[
P_\theta(Z_E\mid G,X)=\frac14\sum_{m=1}^4\prod_{e\in E}q_m(e)^{Z_e}(1-q_m(e))^{1-Z_e}.
\]

This is normalized: summing each component over all binary edge assignments gives ∏_e[q_m(e)+(1−q_m(e))]=1, then the four weights sum to one. Summing out E\S similarly removes every outside factor, leaving exactly the J mixture on S. Thus overlapping supports have compatible marginals under this **constructed conditional law**, with one global latent member M. Different block responsibilities need not coincide: each conditions M on different evidence. The construction is a finite mixture with edges independent conditional on M, not arbitrary graph dependence. Code does not sample M or materialize this whole graph. The local objective also does not uniquely determine this extension; the formula chooses a particular global coupling.

This argument requires distinct edge coordinates within each support. For distinct query endpoints in a loopless graph, the two residual sides are disjoint edge sets. The implementation groups slots, does not deduplicate by counterpart-edge identity, and permits graph self-loops in its generic graph builder (`graph_ops.py:19–31,81–89`). Actual TRAIN looplessness was not checked. Negative-query self-links are source-rejected (`pilot_data.py:80`). Duplicating one edge inside a block would require tying one variable, rather than multiplying its Bernoulli factor twice and calling that the same graph marginal.

**What the training loss actually supplies.** `pattern_train.py:61` enables training; decoder dropout is .3. The outer node transforms are redrawn across positive/negative calls; depth-zero left/right calls redraw their second full-node transforms; decoder dropout draws masks for individual occurrence rows (`prototype.py:119–120,168–185,197–202`). Repeated occurrences of one missing edge therefore need not share a realized q_m even within the same masked graph and encoder realization. Checkpoint RNG preservation reproduces a particular call during backward; it does not couple different occurrences (`pattern_model.py:12–21`). Deterministic algorithms and matched J/F RNG schedules do not disable these draws. Whether dropout-averaged block laws admit some compatible global coupling is unproved here; source alone supplies no such coupling.

For the deterministic bank, `pattern_objective.py:15–23` is −log P(Z_S|G,X)/|S|. Products of these block marginals, with fixed inverse-support/query weights, have the form of a **weighted marginal composite likelihood**. Overlap and repeated blocks are permissible but induce dependent contributions. Their product is not the normalized whole-graph likelihood, and support normalization does not change that fact. F has the corresponding independent-edge extension using mean-member q; J moves the mixture outside the within-block product.

Actual fitting is a stochastic denoising composite surrogate plus native target BCE (`pattern_train.py:25–43`), rather than maximum likelihood of the displayed global model. No globally pooled responsibility or graph-likelihood normalization is optimized. Serving uses soft native completion and mean raw target logits, without sampling graph worlds or posterior responsibilities; its nonlinear target decoder is not the exact predictive integral under P.

**Context, coverage and identification.** Residual neighbors are exhaustively enumerated from G: no neighbor subsampling or degree cap is introduced (`graph_ops.py:55–89`). Native record masks, target/negative sampling, dropped training tails and query-dependent supports still determine which blocks receive supervision. Edges outside those supports are not directly reconstructed. The same edge can legitimately change q when the mask changes G and its features; these separate conditional distributions are not shown to be conditionals of one unconditional graph law. A simple compatibility warning: if positive binary A/B conditionals make P(A=1|B) constant, the joint must make A independent of B; it cannot simultaneously have P(B=1|A=0)≠P(B=1|A=1). No current fitted incompatibility is asserted—there is no source constraint checking such cross-context relations.

Labels are complete-TRAIN observation membership Z; zero means unobserved, not latent absence (`pattern_teacher.py:15–27,42–46`). A law of true links C would additionally need an observation/missingness model relating C to Z. One graph with repeated masks does not identify that model. Member permutations, duplicate/collapsed components, fixed equal weights, limited block coverage and unobserved high-order joint events also preclude an automatic identifiability claim. Shared parameters align component indices mechanically; they do not prove unique modes or calibrated frequencies. No consistency, uncertainty, novelty or predictive theorem follows from the construction.
